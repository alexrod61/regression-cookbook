# Revision rationale for the formal model section

## Scope
- Target chapter/file: book/13-generalized-poisson.qmd (branch Writing-for-Section-13).
- Exact section(s): replaced `## Data Modelling` with `## The Generalized Poisson Regression Model {#sec-generalized-poisson-data-modelling}` plus subsections `{#sec-generalized-poisson-random-component}`, `{#sec-generalized-poisson-systematic-component}`, `{#sec-generalized-poisson-log-link}`.
- Reference chapters/files reviewed: finished Ch10 GLM presentation (random/systematic/link three-part structure at L2524+/L2597+/L2655+), skill.md sections 6 (self-contained boxes), 7 (mathematical rigour), 12 (smooth transitions); Appendix anchor `#sec-generalized-poisson-distribution` (mind-map contract).

## What was preserved
- The draft's correct choices, kept: log link identical to Chapter 10; single shared dispersion parameter; PMF statement; support-truncation mention with m>=4; Poisson nesting at the zero-dispersion point.
- The draft's sectional role: present the model before estimation.

## What changed
- Notation: the draft used delta for the dispersion parameter and theta for the rate-like parameter, with P(Y_i = y_i | theta_i, delta). The replacement adopts Appendix-C-aligned Consul-Jain notation theta in (-1, 1) for dispersion and lambda_i for the rate-like parameter, with p_Y(y; lambda, theta) per Chapter 2 PMF conventions and operatorname{GPO}.
- Added the three-part regression decomposition with anchors, mirroring finished Chapter 10's GLM presentation (random / systematic / log link) instead of the draft's single undivided block.
- Added the self-contained Definition box (mean, variance, dispersion ratio, direction bullets, zero-count probability exp(-lambda), support truncation, appendix cross-reference).
- Added the systematic-component subsection: linear predictor display with bold x_i and beta; definition of linearity; explicit statement that the simple and extended crabs specifications match finished Chapter 10 for apples-to-apples comparison.
- Added the lambda_i = (1 - theta) mu_i identity displaying exactly how the link enters the likelihood via the rate-like parameter (#eq-generalized-poisson-lambda-mean).
- Added a Heads-up distinguishing the GP model from a classical Poisson GLM: no IRLS; joint maximum likelihood over (beta, theta); VGAM vglm machinery in R; direct likelihood maximization in Python. This corrects the draft's vocabulary (it silently treated GP as if it were an ordinary GLM).
- An explicit numerical bridge from theta to the variance multiplier (theta = 0.5 -> ratio 4; theta = -0.5 -> ratio 0.44), concretizing the abstract ratio 1/(1-theta)^2.
- Rewrote the closing paragraph: removed "the interpretation... and their exponentiated IRRs is identical to Chapter 10" (terminology ban and wrong emphasis), and removed the sentence claiming the same model covers both case studies (the B-Line is now a practice-exercise case, not a running case).

## Why the changes help
- Vocabulary and notation consistency with Chapters 1/2/10 and with Appendix C, eliminating the two-variable clash between the draft and the appendix.
- The three-part decomposition and the self-contained definition satisfy skill.md sections 6 and 7 and give later stages the anchors and equations to reference (GOF stage references the zero-count probability and the dispersion ratio).
- The GLM-not-GLM Heads-up prevents a predictable misconception and motivates the estimation section.

## Execution and validation
- No executed computations in this section (formal model presentation); the inline numbers (4x, 0.44x) are dimensionless algebra verified by hand from the dispersion-ratio display.
- Cross-references checked by grep: #sec-generalized-poisson-distribution is the existing appendix anchor family used by the mind map; sec-classical-poisson-glm and sec-classical-poisson-additional-regressors name the finished Chapter 10 anchors referenced in the proposed text.

## What should be avoided
- Reintroducing the P(Y|...) notation or the theta<->lambda swap; calling GP a classical GLM or describing its estimation as IRLS; the acronym IRR; attaching an EM algorithm to this model (EM belongs to mixture models like ZIP).
